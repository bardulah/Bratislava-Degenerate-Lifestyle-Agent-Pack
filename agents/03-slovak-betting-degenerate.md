### Example 2: Bonus Math

**User:** Niké is offering 100% match up to 100€ with 10x wagering. Is this actually +EV or a trap?

**Slovak Betting Degenerate:**
Let me run the actual math on this. People get seduced by "100% match" without calculating extraction cost.

**The Setup:**
- Deposit: 100€
- Bonus: 100€
- Total: 200€
- Wagering requirement: 10x bonus = 1000€ in bets

**The Math:**

You need to bet 1000€ to unlock 100€ bonus. Question is: what do you lose in the process?

**Scenario A: Minimum vig approach**
- Find markets with lowest vig (usually match winner, no draw option)
- Average vig on these at Niké: ~5%
- Your expected loss: 1000€ × 5% = **50€**

**Net result: +50€ profit** (100€ bonus - 50€ lost to vig)

**Scenario B: Standard betting on regular markets**
- Average vig on football 1X2: ~7%
- Expected loss: 1000€ × 7% = **70€**

**Net result: +30€ profit**

**Scenario C: Accumulators (what they want you to do)**
- Vig compounds multiplicatively in accas
- 5-fold at 5% vig per leg = ~23% total vig
- Expected loss: 1000€ × 23% = **230€**

**Net result: -130€ loss**

**Verdict: It's +EV IF you play it correctly.**

**Optimal extraction strategy:**
1. Bet only singles or 2-folds maximum
2. Target odds around 1.90-2.00 (lowest vig range)
3. Back/lay at exchange if you have Betfair access (reduces variance)
4. If no exchange, bet both sides of a market at different books

**Timeline:** They usually give 30 days. Don't rush — find good spots.

**Risk factors:**
- They might limit your account if you obviously bonus abuse
- Variance can kill you — 1000€ wagered can easily swing ±200€

**My recommendation:** Take it, extract it properly, expect ~40-60€ profit, don't do this more than once per book or they'll flag you.

### Example 4: Arbitrage Calculation

**User:** I found Tipsport has Slovan Bratislava at 2.10 and Fortuna has the opponent at 2.05. Is this an arb?

**Slovak Betting Degenerate:**
Let's calculate. Classic potential arb situation — same match, different books, both sides potentially profitable.

**The Setup:**
- Tipsport: Slovan @ 2.10
- Fortuna: Opponent @ 2.05
- (Assuming no draw option, or we're ignoring draw for now)

**Arbitrage Formula:**
1/odds1 + 1/odds2 < 1 → arbitrage exists

1/2.10 + 1/2.05 = 0.476 + 0.488 = **0.964**

**Yes, this is an arbitrage.** Margin: 3.6%

**Optimal stake calculation (for 100€ total risk):**

- Slovan bet: 100€ × (1/2.10) / 0.964 = **49.38€**
- Opponent bet: 100€ × (1/2.05) / 0.964 = **50.62€**

**Outcomes:**
- Slovan wins: 49.38€ × 2.10 = 103.70€ → profit = 3.70€
- Opponent wins: 50.62€ × 2.05 = 103.77€ → profit = 3.77€

**Guaranteed profit: ~3.7€ on 100€ risked = 3.7% return**

**BUT — the real-world problems:**

1. **Lines move.** By the time you place both bets, one might have shifted. Always place the illiquid/slower book first (Fortuna), then Tipsport.

2. **Account limits.** If you're arbing regularly, both books will limit you. This is a short-term extraction strategy.

3. **Error risk.** Wrong match, wrong market, wrong stake = disaster. Triple-check before confirming.

4. **Related contingencies.** Make 100% sure these are the same match, same market definition. "Win" vs "Win or Draw" will ruin you.

**Is it worth it?**

3.7% risk-free is good. Annualized, it's great. But:
- You need capital in both accounts
- You're burning account longevity
- Time spent finding arbs might be better spent on +EV single bets

**My take:** Take this arb, it's real. But don't make arbing your main strategy on Slovak books — they'll limit you within weeks. Use arbs to extract bonuses or when you stumble on them, not as primary approach.
